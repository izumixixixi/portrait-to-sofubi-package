# Flow 1 Variant — dehara (Painted Clay-Sculpt Sofubi)

Use this variant only after the user selects **dehara**. The label names this option in the workflow; the output must be an original person-based toy built from the visual construction rules below, not a copy of any reference character or an artist signature.

## Priority override

This variant overrides the shared Flow 1 rules that require monochrome unpainted vinyl, a 45–50% head, ultra-compact chibi anatomy, only three minimal facial marks, no painted features, no nostril dots, no block teeth, and one uniform selected color. It does not override the source-completeness gate, source pose/crop authority, single-person rule, white studio presentation, or rejection of realistic facial anatomy. Its face target is `simple-vintage-oddball-cute`: more expressive than the pink/cyan blank face but still unmistakably toy-like and non-realistic.

## Sculpt language

Create a **painted hollow sofubi figure cast from a casually hand-shaped clay master**.

- loose, exaggerated, off-kilter human caricature rather than polished designer vinyl
- approximately 3.5–4.5 heads tall, with a large irregular head but still a readable torso and legs
- deliberately uneven left/right volumes and slightly crooked alignment
- simple tube-like torso, cylindrical limbs, blunt mitten hands with optional broad finger grooves, and broad naive feet
- large ears, cheeks, hands, shoes, or one source-specific feature may be exaggerated for charm
- coarse, lumpy volumes, shallow dents, flattened spots, uneven thickness, finger-smoothed transitions, slight waviness, and hand-shaped asymmetry clearly visible beneath the paint
- minimal anatomical modeling; no realistic muscles, joints, facial planes, or precision sculpting
- one or two coarse body or clothing bulges may exaggerate a source-supported build, but never resolve into realistic muscle anatomy
- original pose and gesture from Image 1 remain recognizable even after the loose exaggeration

The clay quality comes from **coarse geometry**, not from a dry material shader. Keep irregular contours and hand-shaped bumps clearly readable after molding and painting, but make the finished object unmistakably glossy soft vinyl. Do not render raw clay, ceramic, plaster, resin, wood, or stop-motion modeling material.

## Rotatable shoulder construction

Both arms must be separate molded parts from the torso. Show a narrow, believable circular swivel seam at each shoulder where the upper arm inserts into the body. The seams should imply that the arms can be twisted by hand while the toy currently holds the source pose.

Use simple vintage rotocast construction only: no exposed pegs, ball joints, hinges, mechanical sockets, action-figure articulation, detached limbs, or exploded views. Do not add shoulder seams where the source crop does not show the shoulders.

## Face

Use `face_mode = simple-vintage-oddball-cute`. The face is a coarse, mask-like arrangement of a few bold parts. It should feel funny, awkward, startled, vacant, or cheerfully strange—never handsome, realistic, refined, or horror-oriented.

Choose only the few facial devices that best translate the source:

- oversized protruding white oval eyes with tiny dot pupils, tiny black bead eyes, or simple flat painted ovals; deliberately uneven spacing is welcome
- one large blunt wedge, bulb, or triangular nose; up to two simple punched-dot nostrils are allowed, but no anatomical nose planes
- a tiny painted mouth mark, thick O-shaped lip ring, or crude open mouth cavity
- when an open mouth is used, zero to six widely spaced chunky block teeth; never realistic rows of teeth, gums, tongue, or dental detail
- thick painted eyebrows, two or three short forehead/cheek lines, or one flat scar mark when useful for expression
- simplified glasses as a thick wobbly painted or raised loop
- moustache or beard as one flat graphic patch or one smooth lower-face mass; no strands, stubble, subtle blending, or anatomical beard edge
- large simple ears may be exaggerated as separate rounded slabs

Features should look individually stuck on, poked in, or painted over a coarse clay-master head, with obvious asymmetry and no delicate transitions. Paint is allowed and expected. Automatically reject realistic eye anatomy, subtle skin shading, anatomical nose modeling, sculpted natural lips, realistic dentition, pores, fine wrinkles, facial planes, portrait-bust likeness, or detailed facial hair.

## Hand-painted color

Use a small, lively, high-saturation palette derived from the source person's visible skin, hair, clothing, shoes, and important accessories—not from any one reference figure. Unexpected color contrast is welcome, but the result must still correspond to the source.

- thick opaque flat hand-painted color blocks with clear separation between skin, hair, clothing, and accessories
- simplified source color relationships rather than literal textile reproduction
- deliberately wobbly paint edges, small registration shifts, minor overlaps, uneven line widths, and visible hand-placement irregularity
- one or two simplified painted motifs only when they correspond to a visible source feature
- slight density variation from hand application, but no dirt, damage, heavy distressing, or accidental-looking smears
- no copied tie pattern, underwear, tattoo, body paint, costume, logo, text, or color scheme from the references

The paint should follow and emphasize the coarse sculpt instead of hiding it. Graphic paint may simplify hairlines, eyebrows, moustaches, eyes, lips, collars, ties, belts, pockets, or one source-supported garment motif. Avoid airbrushed realism, subtle gradients, photoreal fabric prints, fine linework, and clean vector-perfect masking.

## Sofubi material

The underlying object is real hollow soft vinyl:

- softly rounded molded edges and slightly yielding visual weight
- small logical mold seams plus the two readable shoulder swivel seams
- low surface roughness with an unmistakable medium-to-high-gloss sofubi response
- broad rounded white-core highlights and smaller hotspots that travel continuously across hand-shaped bumps, dents, paint boundaries, head, shoulders, torso, hands, and shoes
- strong specular response over both base vinyl and painted areas; small paint regions may vary slightly, but the whole object remains glossy
- clean studio reflections and soft form shadows that expose the rough geometry; never matte, chalky, powdery, dry, ceramic, or raw-clay looking

Do not use the uniform single-color blank treatment of the pink/cyan variants. The dehara result must feel coarsely hand-sculpted and hand-painted **under a strong glossy sofubi finish**. Gloss is not optional and must not smooth away the sculpt's rough, lumpy shape.

## Presentation and acceptance

Photograph exactly one finished toy on the same clean white or light-neutral studio background required by the shared prompt. Do not reproduce any reference environment.

Accept only when the result:

- is clearly the person from Image 1 through pose, outfit blocks, headwear/hair, and accessories
- has loose, exaggerated, casually hand-shaped proportions rather than polished chibi or realistic anatomy
- shows coarse, lumpy, asymmetric clay-master geometry beneath saturated hand-painted color blocks
- has both arms built as separate shoulder-swivel parts whenever both shoulders are visible
- keeps the face simple, vintage, cute, and non-realistic
- preserves strong glossy soft-vinyl highlights across both paint and irregular molded forms
- contains no copied reference character, monster/animal trait, branding, text, environment, extra figure, or unrelated prop

Judge three axes separately: coarse sculpt geometry, hand-painted color treatment, and glossy sofubi material. Never accept a result that satisfies only two. If the sculpt is too smooth, correct only asymmetry, lumpy volumes, shallow dents, flattened spots, and clay-master waviness. If paint looks digital, correct only flat color blocks, wobbly edges, registration shifts, and line irregularity. If the surface looks matte or like raw clay, correct only the strong rounded vinyl highlights and high-gloss response. Correct shoulder construction separately when needed. Preserve every already-approved axis, the source pose and content, face, framing, and background.
